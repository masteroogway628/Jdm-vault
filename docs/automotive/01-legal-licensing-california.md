# Legal, Licensing & Compliance — California

Everything you must have in place, in the order you should get it. Statutes are cited
so you can verify each item yourself. **Verify current fees and thresholds directly
with the agency — they change annually.**

---

## 1. Business structure

### Entity choice

**Recommendation: California LLC, member-managed.**

| Entity | Verdict for this business |
| --- | --- |
| Sole proprietorship | **No.** You are working on other people's cars, under them, with lifts and fire. Unlimited personal liability is disqualifying. |
| **LLC** | **Yes.** Liability shield, cheap to run, flexible tax treatment, credible to landlords and insurers. |
| S-corp (entity) | Not at first. More formality, payroll required from day one. |
| C-corp | No, unless you're raising outside investment. Double taxation. |

**The move most people miss:** form the LLC, then elect S-corp *tax* treatment (IRS
Form 2553) once net profit reliably exceeds roughly **$60,000–70,000/year**. Below that
threshold, the payroll administration and additional CA taxes eat the savings. Above it,
you split income between reasonable W-2 salary and distributions, and the distributions
avoid the 15.3% self-employment tax. On $120K of net profit this is commonly a
$6,000–9,000/year swing. Run the actual numbers with a CPA before electing.

### Formation steps and real costs

| Step | Agency | Cost | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name availability check | CA Secretary of State | Free | Day 1 |
| Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) | CA SoS | ~$70 | Day 1, online |
| Operating Agreement | Yourself/attorney | $0–1,500 | Week 1 |
| EIN | IRS | **Free** — never pay for this | Same day, online |
| Statement of Information (LLC-12) | CA SoS | ~$20 | **Within 90 days**, then every 2 years |
| Business bank account | Bank | $0–25/mo | Week 1 |
| City business license | Your city | $50–500/yr | Before opening |
| Seller's Permit | CDTFA | Free | Before selling parts |

**Multi-member warning.** If you're going in with a friend — extremely common in car
businesses, and the most common way they end — the Operating Agreement must address:
capital contributions, who works in the business and for what pay, decision thresholds,
what happens when someone stops showing up, buy-sell terms, and death/divorce. Pay an
attorney $1,000–2,000 for this. The friendship is worth more than the savings.

### DBA / Fictitious Business Name

Required when you operate under a name that isn't your registered LLC name. "Vault
Motorworks LLC" doing business as "The Vault" needs a DBA.

- File with the **county clerk** where your principal place of business sits
- File **within 40 days** of first using the name
- **Publish** in an adjudicated newspaper of general circulation, once a week for
  **four successive weeks**, then file proof of publication
- Renew every **5 years**
- Cost: roughly **$26–60 filing + $30–100 publication**

*Authority: Business & Professions Code §§17900 et seq.*

Practical note: a bank will usually not deposit checks made out to a name you haven't
registered. File the DBA before you print anything.

## 2. Insurance — the part that decides whether you have a business

This is where most first-time shop owners are dangerously underinsured. Read this
section twice.

### The coverages, and what each actually does

| Coverage | What it covers | Why you need it | Est. annual cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Garage Liability** | Bodily injury/property damage from your operations | The base policy for any auto business | $1,800–5,000 |
| **Garagekeepers Legal Liability** | Damage to **customer vehicles in your care** | Your GL does **not** cover the customer's car. This is the one people skip and get destroyed by. | $800–3,000 |
| **Commercial Property / BPP** | Your tools, equipment, tenant improvements | Tools walk. Shops burn. | $600–2,500 |
| **Workers' Compensation** | Employee injury | **Mandatory in California with even one employee.** Non-negotiable. | Class 8391 ≈ $3–6 per $100 of payroll |
| **Commercial Auto** | Your vehicles, test drives | Personal auto policies exclude business use | $1,200–3,500 |
| **Products & Completed Operations** | Failure of work after the car leaves | **Critical for performance installs.** Turbo kit fails at 3,000 miles = claim. | Often endorsed onto GL |
| **Dealer's Open Lot** | Physical damage to sale inventory | Required once you hold inventory | $1,500–4,000 |
| **Umbrella** | Excess over the above | $1M primary is not much in a California injury case | $800–2,500 |
| **Tools & Equipment (inland marine)** | Tools off-premises, mobile ops | Phase 0 mobile work | $300–900 |
| **EPLI** | Employment claims | Once you have 3+ employees | $800–2,000 |

**Realistic total for a small shop with 1–3 people: $6,000–14,000/year.** Budget
$900–1,200/month. If someone quotes you $200/month, read the exclusions — you are
almost certainly missing garagekeepers or completed operations.

### Three critical insurance realities

**1. Garagekeepers has two forms, and the difference matters enormously.**
*Legal liability* form pays only if you're proven negligent. *Direct primary* form pays
for damage to the customer's car regardless of fault. A hailstorm or a theft off your
lot is not your negligence — legal liability form pays nothing, and you still have an
angry customer and a $40,000 car. **Buy direct primary.** It costs meaningfully more.
Buy it anyway.

**2. DIY bay rental is a coverage problem, not a paperwork problem.**
A member injured working on their own car under your roof is bodily injury to a
non-employee on your premises. Standard garage policies commonly exclude it. You need a
carrier that specifically writes DIY/self-service garage exposure. Expect:
- Substantially higher premiums, if you can place it at all as a new venture
- Underwriter requirements: written safety rules, mandatory orientation, staff on duty
  whenever bays are occupied, equipment inspection logs, no working alone
- A signed **waiver and assumption of risk** for every user, every visit — understanding
  that California courts will not enforce a waiver for gross negligence, and never for
  a defective lift you failed to inspect

**Do not open member bays until you have that policy in writing.** "I have garage
liability, it's probably fine" is how this business ends.

**3. Tell your broker the truth about what you do.**
Material misrepresentation on the application voids the policy at claim time — which is
precisely when you find out. If you do wraps, welding, dyno work, performance tuning,
storage, or events, disclose all of it. Use a broker who specializes in automotive
risks, not your personal-lines agent.

## 3. Taxes

### California entity-level taxes

| Tax | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LLC annual franchise tax** | **$800/year minimum** | Due whether or not you profit. Confirm current first-year rules with FTB. |
| **LLC gross receipts fee** | Tiered — commonly ~$900 at $250K, ~$2,500 at $500K, ~$6,000 at $1M | Based on **gross receipts, not profit** |
| **Form 568** | Annual LLC return | Confirm current fee schedule in the 568 instructions |
| Federal | Pass-through to your 1040, or S-corp payroll + K-1 | |

**The gross receipts fee is a trap for car flippers.** If you sell 30 cars at $18,000,
your gross receipts are $540,000 even though your gross *profit* might be $60,000. You
owe the fee tier on the $540,000. Model this before you scale flipping volume — it can
make a high-volume, thin-margin flipping business worse than a low-volume, fat-margin
service business at the same take-home.

### Sales & use tax — the rules that specifically affect you

Register for a **Seller's Permit** with CDTFA (free) before selling any parts.

The distinction that matters most in an automotive shop:

| Type of labor | Taxable in California? |
| --- | --- |
| **Repair / installation labor** | **Not taxable** |
| **Fabrication / assembly labor** | **Taxable** |
| Parts and materials | **Taxable** |

That means when you build a custom exhaust, roll cage, or one-off bracket, **the labor
is taxable** because you're creating a new item. When you install a customer's
off-the-shelf catback, the labor isn't. Get this wrong for three years and a CDTFA audit
assesses the tax plus penalties and interest out of your pocket.

Other essentials:
- **Resale certificates** let you buy parts and (once licensed) vehicles tax-free for
  resale. This is the single largest economic advantage of a dealer license.
- **Use tax** applies to anything you buy out of state without tax and use in the
  business — including tools bought online. Audited routinely.
- **Keep parts markup and labor separately stated on every invoice.** Bundling them
  makes the entire invoice vulnerable in an audit.

### Bookkeeping — set this up in week one

- Separate business bank account and card, from day one. Commingling is how you lose
  the LLC's liability shield ("piercing the veil").
- QuickBooks or Wave, reconciled monthly. $30–90/mo.
- A CPA who has automotive clients. $150–400/mo, or $1,200–2,500 at tax time.
- Retain records **at least 4 years** (CDTFA), longer for BAR and DMV requirements.
- Track **per-car profitability** if you flip, and **per-job profitability** if you
  service. Shops die from not knowing which jobs lose money.

## 4. Automotive licenses and permits

### 4a. BAR — Automotive Repair Dealer registration (you almost certainly need this)

**Anyone who repairs or diagnoses vehicles for compensation in California must register
with the Bureau of Automotive Repair as an Automotive Repair Dealer (ARD).**

This catches far more people than they expect. If you take money to work on someone
else's car — including from a bay renter whose car you help with — you need an ARD.

*Authority: Business & Professions Code §§9880 et seq.*

- Registration per location; fee is modest (budget roughly $200/year — **verify current
  amount**)
- Display your ARD number on signage, invoices, and estimates
- **Written estimate required before work begins.** You may not exceed the estimate
  without the customer's documented authorization. This is the #1 source of BAR
  complaints and enforcement.
- Return replaced parts to the customer on request
- Invoices must itemize parts and labor, and identify used or rebuilt parts
- BAR can and does perform undercover operations

**Separate licenses if you do these:**

| Activity | License needed |
| --- | --- |
| Smog inspections | Smog Check Station license + licensed technician |
| Brake/lamp certificates | Brake and Lamp Adjuster station + technician |
| Dismantling cars for parts | **Dismantler license** (CVC §220) — different from a dealer license |
| Selling vehicles | DMV dealer license (below) |

**Note on the hobby-shop model:** if members do their own work and you take no
compensation for repair services, the ARD analysis is different — but the moment your
staff touches a car for money, you're an ARD. In practice you will do both. Register.

### 4b. DMV Dealer License — the section to read carefully

#### How many can you sell without one?

**This is the most misreported rule on the internet, so here is how to think about it
correctly.**

California defines a "dealer" primarily by **conduct and intent**, not solely by a
count. Under **CVC §285**, a dealer is generally a person who sells or offers vehicles
for profit, or who is engaged wholly or in part in the business of selling vehicles.
**CVC §286** lists exclusions.

The number commonly cited by the DMV and industry as the personal-sale ceiling is
**five vehicles in a 12-month period, where each vehicle was registered in your own
name.** ⚠️ **Verify this figure directly with DMV Occupational Licensing before you
rely on it** — it is the single most consequential number in this document.

What matters more than the count, and what actually gets people cited:

- **Intent controls.** Buying a car *for the purpose of resale* is dealer conduct. The
  DMV has prosecuted people over fewer than five vehicles where the pattern showed
  intent to resell for profit.
- **The car must actually be titled in your name.** Selling a car you never registered
  — signing over a title you received open — is **title jumping**, illegal, and the
  hallmark of curbstoning.
- **Advertising is evidence.** Multiple concurrent listings, a business name, a
  consistent phone number across ads, or selling from a commercial lot all establish
  that you're operating as a dealer.

**Curbstoning penalties:** operating without a license violates **CVC §11700** and is
a misdemeanor, with fines, possible jail, vehicle impound, and exposure under Business
& Professions Code §17200 (unfair competition). DMV Investigations runs sting
operations. This is enforced, not theoretical.

**The practical takeaway:** the personal threshold is for *testing the model*, not for
*running a business*. If flipping is your plan, get licensed early — and the tax math
below means you'd want to anyway.

#### The tax math that should drive your decision

| | Unlicensed private flipper | Licensed dealer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying inventory | **Pay use tax** on registration (~7.75–10.75%) | **Tax-free on resale certificate** |
| Auction access | Public auctions only | **Manheim, Copart, IAA, ADESA** |
| Per-car tax cost on a $7,000 buy | **$540–750 destroyed margin** | $0 |
| Financing inventory | Personal cash | Floor plan lines available |
| Ceiling | ~5 cars/year | Unlimited |

On a flip with $2,000 of gross profit, the use tax alone consumes 25–37% of it. **That
is the argument for the license, and it is stronger than the volume argument.**

#### Dealer license types

| Type | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| **Used vehicle dealer (retail)** | Sell to the public. What you want. |
| **Wholesale-only** | Sell only to other licensed dealers. Cheaper, lighter facility requirements, no retail. |
| **Autobroker endorsement** | Arrange purchases on a buyer's behalf. Add-on to a dealer license. |
| **Dismantler** | Part out vehicles. Separate license entirely. |

A common on-ramp: **start wholesale-only** (lower facility bar, auction access) while
you build capital, then convert to retail. Confirm current requirements for each class
with DMV OL.

#### Requirements to get licensed

1. **Pre-licensing education.** DMV-approved dealer education course (commonly ~6 hours)
   and pass the DMV dealer exam. Course cost roughly $100–300.
2. **Surety bond.** Under **CVC §11710**, retail used-vehicle dealers are generally
   required to post a **$50,000 bond**; a reduced bond (commonly cited as $10,000) may
   apply to dealers selling fewer than 25 vehicles per year, and motorcycle dealers.
   **Verify your class's amount.**
   - You don't pay $50,000 — you pay an annual premium.
   - Good credit: roughly **$400–1,500/year**. Poor credit: **$1,500–5,000/year**.
   - The bond protects *customers*, not you. If it pays out, the surety comes after you
     for the full amount.
3. **Established place of business.** See facility requirements below.
4. **Live Scan fingerprinting** and background check for every owner/officer.
5. **Application package** — Original Occupational License application, personal history
   questionnaire, photos of the premises, proof of zoning approval, city business
   license, seller's permit.
6. **DMV inspector site inspection.** An inspector visits and verifies the premises meet
   requirements before issuance.
7. **Fees.** Application fee, dealer plates (each), branch locations. Budget roughly
   **$400–800 in DMV fees**, plus everything else.

**Realistic all-in cost to become a licensed retail used dealer: $3,000–7,000**, not
counting rent, inventory, or lot improvements.

#### Established place of business — physical requirements

DMV requires a genuine business location, not a house. Generally:

- **Office space** with a desk, phone listed in the business name, and secure record
  storage
- **Display area** sufficient to display vehicles offered for sale — the lot must
  actually be able to hold inventory
- **Permanent exterior sign** legible from the street, showing the business name, with
  minimum letter dimensions specified by regulation — **confirm the current dimensional
  requirement**
- **Posted business hours**, and you must actually be there during them
- **Zoning compliance** for vehicle sales at that address, certified by the city
- Records available at the location for DMV inspection

You cannot run a licensed dealership from a residence or a shared mailbox. This is
where the capital commitment becomes real, and it's why phase 3 comes last in the plan.

#### Ongoing dealer obligations

- **Report of Sale** submitted to DMV for every retail sale, within the required
  timeframe, with **temporary paper license plates** issued to the buyer (required since
  AB 516)
- **Collect and remit sales tax** at the buyer's rate
- **Contract cancellation option** must be offered on used vehicles under the statutory
  price threshold (Car Buyer's Bill of Rights — **CVC §11713.21**). This is a 2-day
  return right the buyer may purchase. Offering it is mandatory; buying it is optional.
- **FTC Buyer's Guide** (window sticker) displayed on every used vehicle, with the
  Spanish version if you negotiate in Spanish
- **Smog certificate** provided at delivery (below)
- **Odometer disclosure** on transfer
- Maintain records for the required retention period, available for DMV inspection
- Advertising rules: no bait-and-switch, disclose the vehicle by stock or VIN in
  price-specific ads

#### Consignment sales

Selling someone else's car for them **requires a dealer license** — you cannot do it as
a service without one. California adds specific protections:

- A **written consignment agreement** with required terms
- Consignor proceeds handled per statute, with the consignor paid within the statutory
  window after sale
- Consignment funds are effectively trust funds — **do not use them for operating
  expenses.** Mixing consignment proceeds into working capital is the fastest route to
  license revocation and criminal exposure.

*Authority: CVC §§11736 et seq. — verify current terms and payment window.*

Consignment is genuinely attractive: **zero inventory capital, 8–12% commission or a
flat $1,500–3,000 per car, no floor-plan risk.** It is the best flipping-adjacent
business for a shop that already has enthusiast customers. It's phase 3 because it needs
the license.

### 4c. Smog requirements

| Situation | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Dealer selling a used vehicle** | **The dealer must provide a valid smog certificate at the time of delivery.** This is your cost, and it's the most common surprise expense on a flip. |
| **Private party sale** | Seller provides a certificate; generally valid if issued within 90 days |
| Vehicles **4 or fewer model years old** | Exempt from the transfer smog requirement (a smog transfer fee applies instead) |
| **Model year 1975 and older** | **Exempt from smog check entirely** |
| Diesel | 1997 and older exempt; also exempt above the GVWR threshold |
| Electric | Exempt |
| Motorcycles | Exempt |
| Family transfers | Certain transfers between family members are exempt |

**Budget $60–120 per car for smog, and budget for failure.** A car that fails is your
repair bill before you can sell it. On cheap flips, a catalytic converter failure can
erase the entire profit — **CARB-compliant converters are required in California and
cost 2–4x the federal part.** Pre-purchase smog testing on any flip candidate is
non-negotiable.

⚠️ Verify current exemption years — the smog exemption cutoff and diesel rules are
periodically revised by the legislature.

### 4d. Title transfer process

**As a private seller (your phase 0 flips):**

1. Sign the title in the correct seller section; complete the **odometer disclosure**
   (federal rules now require disclosure for vehicles up to 20 model years old)
2. Provide a **valid smog certificate** unless exempt
3. Complete a **bill of sale**
4. File a **Notice of Transfer and Release of Liability (REG 138) within 5 days** —
   ⚠️ **do this every single time.** It's free, online, takes two minutes, and it is
   the only thing standing between you and liability for tickets, tolls, impounds, and
   collisions involving a car you no longer own.
5. Buyer must apply for transfer **within 10 days** (**CVC §5753**) or face penalties

**As a buyer:** apply for title within 10 days, pay transfer fee and use tax. If the
title is missing, you need a duplicate title application from the registered owner — a
seller who can't produce a title is a deal you walk away from, every time.

**As a licensed dealer:** submit the Report of Sale, issue temporary plates, and submit
the transfer application within the required timeframe. Most dealers use an electronic
DMV Business Partner Automation provider or a registration service — budget **$150–350
per transaction** for a registration service, or the setup cost of BPA access.

**Never accept or pass an "open title."** Title jumping is illegal, voids your ability
to prove chain of ownership, and is the defining marker of curbstoning.

## 5. Zoning and land use

**Check zoning before you sign anything. Not after. Verbally from the landlord is not
checking.**

### What you're looking for

| Zone | Typical suitability |
| --- | --- |
| **M-1 / Light Industrial** | Best fit. Repair, fabrication, storage usually permitted by right. |
| **M-2 / Heavy Industrial** | Works, often cheaper, may be far from customers. |
| **C-M / Commercial Manufacturing** | Often good. |
| **C-2 / General Commercial** | Repair often needs a **Conditional Use Permit**; sales sometimes permitted. |
| **C-1 / Neighborhood Commercial** | Usually not. |
| **Residential** | **No.** See below. |

### The process

1. Get the **parcel's zoning designation** from the city planning department — by
   address, in writing or by email
2. Ask specifically: *is automotive repair permitted by right, by CUP, or prohibited?*
   And separately: *is vehicle sales/display permitted?* **They are different uses with
   different approvals.** A unit zoned for repair may not allow a dealer license.
3. If a **Conditional Use Permit** is required: budget **$1,500–8,000 in fees and
   3–9 months.** Public hearing. Neighbors can object. This can kill a location.
4. Obtain a **Certificate of Occupancy** for your use before opening
5. Fire department clearance and permits (below)
6. Sign permit from the city — separate from your lease

### Lease clauses to negotiate

- **Zoning/use contingency:** lease is void if the city won't approve your use. Non-
  negotiable ask.
- **Permitted use clause** written broadly enough to cover everything you plan: repair,
  fabrication, detailing, storage, sales, events.
- Landlord consent for **lift installation** and floor anchoring, in writing
- **Sign rights** on the building and the monument sign
- Who pays for **compliance upgrades** (ADA, fire, electrical) if the city requires them
- **Personal guaranty** — try to cap it at 6–12 months' rent rather than the full term
- Early-termination or assignment rights

### Running it from your home garage

Direct answer: **you can start here, but you cannot run a real business here for long.**

- Most California cities' **home occupation permits** prohibit auto repair outright,
  prohibit customer traffic, prohibit outdoor work, and prohibit employees
- You cannot get a **DMV dealer license** at a residence
- HOAs and code enforcement respond to neighbor complaints, and neighbors complain
  about noise, fluids, and parked cars
- Your homeowner's policy **excludes business activity** — a fire started by your work
  can be denied

**Legitimate uses of a home garage:** your own project cars, your own flips under the
personal threshold, and storing tools while you run a *mobile* service business. That's
it. The mobile phase-0 model in the README works precisely because it puts the work at
the customer's location, not yours.

## 6. Environmental compliance

California enforces this seriously, and fines are per-day. This is not optional
paperwork.

### Hazardous waste

You will generate: **used oil, oil filters, antifreeze, brake fluid, solvents, used
absorbents, batteries, tires, and aerosol cans.**

- Obtain an **EPA/DTSC hazardous waste generator ID number**
- Use a **licensed hazardous waste hauler** and keep **manifests** — you are liable for
  your waste forever, including at the disposal site ("cradle to grave")
- **Used oil** must be recycled, stored in labeled, closed, secondary-contained
  containers
- **Universal waste** (batteries, lamps, e-waste) has its own handling rules
- **Never** pour anything down a drain, a storm drain, or onto the ground. This is the
  violation that produces criminal referrals, not just fines.

Budget: **$100–400/month** for waste pickup at small-shop volume.

### CUPA and Hazardous Materials Business Plan

Your county **Certified Unified Program Agency** (usually the fire department or
environmental health) is your primary inspector.

- A **Hazardous Materials Business Plan** is required once you store hazardous materials
  above threshold quantities — commonly cited as **55 gallons of liquid, 500 pounds of
  solid, or 200 cubic feet of compressed gas**. ⚠️ Verify with your CUPA.
- Filed electronically through the state **CERS** system, updated annually
- Expect **annual inspections**. Fees typically **$200–1,500/year**.
- Call your CUPA *before* you open and ask what applies. They vastly prefer helping you
  set up correctly to fining you later, and that call is free.

### Stormwater

- Auto **dismantling and salvage** operations generally require coverage under the
  **Industrial General Permit**. Repair shops often qualify for a **No Exposure
  Certification** if all activities and materials are indoors.
- **Keep everything under roof.** No outdoor fluid changes, no leaking cars over open
  pavement, no uncovered waste containers.
- **Wash water from detailing may not enter the storm drain.** This is the most commonly
  violated rule in the detailing business. You need either a wash bay plumbed to the
  **sanitary sewer** (with a permit and often a **clarifier/sand-oil separator** from
  your local wastewater authority) or a **containment mat with reclaim/haul-off** for
  mobile work.
- Mobile detailers: a containment mat and a vacuum recovery system are the cost of doing
  this legally. **$800–3,000.**

### Air quality

Your local air district (SCAQMD, BAAQMD, SJVAPCD, etc.) regulates:

- **Spray booths and coatings** — a paint booth requires a permit, VOC-compliant
  coatings, and filtration. Rules on automotive coatings and solvent cleaning are strict.
  **This is why "no paint booth" is in the phase-1 plan.**
- **Abrasive blasting** — permit typically required
- Welding is generally fine at small scale with ventilation
- **Ceramic coating and PPF are excellent partly because they largely avoid this**

Budget for a booth: **$25,000–60,000 equipment plus permitting, and months of process.**
Subcontract paint work until you're much larger.

### Proposition 65

Post required warnings. Automotive service facilities have specific safe-harbor warning
language for chemicals encountered in repair work. Signage cost is trivial; the fine for
not having it isn't. Check OEHHA's current safe-harbor language.

### Other

- **Tires:** waste tire hauler/generator requirements and fees
- **Refrigerant (A/C work):** EPA Section 609 technician certification required, and
  approved recovery equipment
- **Aboveground fuel/oil storage** over threshold volumes triggers **SPCC** planning

## 7. Cal/OSHA

California runs its own OSHA, stricter than federal. With **even one employee**, all of
this applies.

### The mandatory foundation

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Injury & Illness Prevention Program (IIPP)** | **Title 8 §3203.** Written, site-specific, with a named responsible person, hazard assessment, inspections, training records, and corrective action. **Every California employer must have one.** It is the first thing an inspector asks for, and not having it is an automatic citation. |
| **Workers' Compensation** | Mandatory with one employee. Operating without it is a criminal offense and exposes you personally. |
| **Hazard Communication** | Chemical inventory, Safety Data Sheets accessible, labeled containers, employee training |
| **Log 300** | Injury recordkeeping; post the annual summary Feb 1–Apr 30 |
| **Serious injury reporting** | Report to Cal/OSHA **within 8 hours** of a serious injury or fatality |
| **Emergency Action Plan / fire prevention** | Exits, extinguishers (inspected annually), evacuation |
| **PPE** | Eye protection, gloves, hearing protection where required — provided by the employer |
| **Eyewash station** | Required where corrosives are handled |
| **Lockout/Tagout** | For equipment servicing |
| **Respiratory protection** | If spraying, sanding, or blasting — includes fit testing and medical evaluation |
| **Heat illness prevention** | For outdoor work — water, shade, rest, training |

### Automotive-specific

- **Vehicle lifts must be inspected annually** by a qualified inspector (ANSI/ALI
  standard). **Keep the certificates.** After a lift accident, the inspection record is
  the difference between a covered claim and a ruinous one.
- Lift operator training documented for anyone who uses one
- Compressed gas cylinders secured upright, caps on when not in use
- Welding: screens, ventilation, fire watch, hot work procedures
- Jack stands rated and used — always, including by you
- Adequate ventilation for running engines; CO monitoring

### Practical advice

Cal/OSHA offers a **free, confidential on-site consultation** service that does not
issue citations. Use it before you have employees. It's the cheapest compliance
investment available.

Budget: **$500–2,000** for an initial IIPP, signage, extinguishers, eyewash, first aid,
and PPE.

## 8. Employees vs. independent contractors

**California's rule is the strictest in the country, and the automotive industry is a
known enforcement target. Assume everyone working in your shop is an employee.**

### The ABC test

Codified at **Labor Code §2775** (from AB 5 / *Dynamex*). A worker is an **employee**
unless the hiring entity proves **all three**:

- **(A)** The worker is free from control and direction in performing the work, both
  contractually and in fact; **and**
- **(B)** The work is **outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business**;
  **and**
- **(C)** The worker is customarily engaged in an independently established trade,
  occupation, or business of the same nature.

**Prong B is fatal for shops.** A mechanic performing repairs at a repair shop is
performing work squarely within the usual course of the business. There is no
paperwork, no signed agreement, and no 1099 that fixes this. A "contractor" mechanic
working your bays on your schedule with your equipment is your employee.

### What that costs if you get it wrong

- Back wages, unpaid overtime, meal and rest period premiums
- Unpaid payroll taxes plus penalties and interest (EDD)
- **Willful misclassification penalties of $5,000–$25,000 per violation** (Labor Code
  §226.8)
- Workers' comp premium fraud exposure — and an **uninsured injury claim you pay
  personally**
- Wage statement penalties, PAGA exposure

A single misclassified mechanic who gets hurt can end the business.

### Who *can* legitimately be a contractor

Genuinely independent businesses performing work outside your usual course: your
bookkeeper, your CPA, a marketing agency, an IT consultant, the electrician who wires
your lift. There is a **business-to-business exemption (Labor Code §2776)** with roughly
a dozen conditions — including that the contractor has its own business location,
business license, its own clients, and is free from your control. It is narrow. Do not
self-diagnose your way into it.

### The specialist-vendor question you will face

You'll be tempted to bring in a tint installer or wrap installer as a "contractor."
Two structures that are more defensible than a 1099:

1. **Hire them as an employee**, part-time, commission-based. Clean, and it's what the
   law contemplates.
2. **Sublease space to a genuinely separate business** that has its own LLC, its own ARD
   registration, its own insurance naming you as additional insured, its own clients,
   its own tools, sets its own prices, and invoices its own customers directly. That is
   a landlord-tenant relationship, not an employment one — but it only works if it's
   *real*. If you book their jobs, set their prices, and pay them a cut, it's
   employment.

**Have an employment attorney review your structure before your first hire.** $500–1,500
for that review is the best money in this plan.

### Once you have employees

- Register with **EDD** within 20 days of paying $100+ in wages
- Payroll service (Gusto, ADP): **$40–150/month.** Do not do payroll by hand in
  California.
- Required posters, wage notices (Labor Code §2810.5), sick leave accrual
- **Meal and rest breaks** tracked and documented — the highest-volume wage claim in
  California
- Overtime after 8 hours/day and 40 hours/week (California is a daily-overtime state)
- **CalSavers** retirement program registration if you don't offer a plan
- Final pay rules: terminated employees must be paid **immediately**

## 9. Compliance checklist by phase

### Phase 0 — Mobile service, no lease
- [ ] LLC formed, EIN, Statement of Information, operating agreement
- [ ] Business bank account and bookkeeping
- [ ] DBA filed and published if using a trade name
- [ ] City business license (and each city you operate in — many require it)
- [ ] **BAR ARD registration** if performing any repair for compensation
- [ ] Seller's permit (CDTFA)
- [ ] Commercial general/garage liability + garagekeepers + tools coverage
- [ ] Commercial auto on your work vehicle
- [ ] Water containment for any washing

### Phase 1 — Industrial unit
- [ ] Zoning verified in writing **before signing**
- [ ] Certificate of Occupancy
- [ ] Fire department permits and inspection
- [ ] Sign permit
- [ ] CUPA registration / HMBP if over threshold; CERS filing
- [ ] Hazardous waste generator ID + hauler contract
- [ ] Stormwater: No Exposure Certification or IGP coverage
- [ ] Wash water plumbed to sewer with permit, or containment
- [ ] **Written IIPP**, extinguishers, eyewash, first aid, SDS binder, Prop 65 signage
- [ ] Lift permit, professional install, **annual ALI inspection scheduled**
- [ ] Upgraded garage liability + garagekeepers direct primary + property + completed ops
- [ ] Workers' comp before the first employee's first hour

### Phase 2 — Storage and member bays
- [ ] **Specialty DIY-garage liability policy bound in writing**
- [ ] Waiver and assumption of risk, attorney-drafted
- [ ] Written safety rules, mandatory orientation, staff-on-duty policy
- [ ] Storage agreements (bailment terms, insurance responsibility, lien rights)
- [ ] Event/special event permit and event liability rider for meets
- [ ] Verify storage and assembly uses are within your zoning and occupancy

### Phase 3 — Dealer license
- [ ] Zoning approval for **vehicle sales** specifically
- [ ] Established place of business meeting DMV physical requirements
- [ ] Dealer education course + exam passed
- [ ] Surety bond issued
- [ ] Live Scan completed for all owners
- [ ] Application submitted; DMV site inspection passed
- [ ] Dealer plates issued
- [ ] Dealer's open lot insurance
- [ ] Report of Sale / temporary plate process and BPA or registration service
- [ ] Consignment agreement and **separate trust handling** for consignor funds
- [ ] Sales tax collection configured
