# Location, Business Models & Startup Budgets

All dollar figures are 2025–2026 California estimates. Coastal metros run high, inland
runs low. Get live quotes before budgeting.

### California rent reality by region

Industrial space, **per square foot per month, NNN basis**:

| Region | Base rent/sf/mo | NNN add | 2,000 sf all-in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LA County (SFV, SGV) | $1.35–2.10 | $0.15–0.35 | **$3,000–4,900** |
| Orange County | $1.50–2.25 | $0.20–0.40 | **$3,400–5,300** |
| San Diego | $1.60–2.30 | $0.20–0.40 | **$3,600–5,400** |
| **Inland Empire** | **$0.95–1.60** | $0.15–0.30 | **$2,200–3,800** |
| Sacramento | $0.90–1.35 | $0.15–0.30 | **$2,100–3,300** |
| Central Valley (Fresno, Bakersfield) | $0.70–1.15 | $0.10–0.25 | **$1,600–2,800** |

**NNN means you also pay taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance on top of base
rent.** A quoted "$1.20/sf" unit is really $1.35–1.50 all-in. Always ask for the NNN
estimate in writing.

**Market note:** the Inland Empire saw significant industrial softening through 2024–25
after years of extreme tightness. Vacancy is up and landlords are giving concessions —
free rent periods of 1–3 months and TI allowances are negotiable in a way they weren't
in 2022. **Ask for free rent. The worst answer is no.** Two months free on a
$3,000 unit is $6,000 of working capital you didn't have to raise.

### What to actually look for in a unit

Non-negotiables:
- **Ceiling height 14 ft clear minimum** — you cannot install a two-post lift under 12
  ft, and 14 ft is comfortable. Measure to the lowest obstruction, not the peak.
- **Concrete thickness for lifts** — most two-post lifts require roughly **4 inches of
  3,000+ psi concrete**, and manufacturers specify exact requirements. Ask the landlord;
  if unknown, a core sample costs $300–600 and is worth it. Thin or cracked slab means
  cutting and pouring footings — $2,000–5,000.
- **Ground-level roll-up door**, 12 ft wide minimum, 14 ft tall preferred
- **Adequate power** — 200A service, and **240V single-phase minimum**. Three-phase is
  better and cheaper to run for compressors and welders. Adding three-phase later can
  cost $5,000–20,000+ from the utility.
- **Zoning verified in writing** before signing (see doc 01)

Strong preferences:
- Corner or end-cap unit — better visibility, fewer shared-wall neighbors
- Dedicated parking, ideally 6+ spaces
- Fenced or gated yard, even a small one
- A floor drain — but **understand what it's connected to**; an unpermitted drain to
  storm is a liability you inherit
- Existing office and restroom (building one out is $8,000–25,000)

### Parking — the constraint nobody plans for

This kills more automotive leases than zoning does. Cities impose parking minimums by
use, commonly something like **one space per 300–500 sf** for industrial plus dedicated
spaces per service bay. Meanwhile:

- Every car in for service occupies a space, sometimes for days
- Cars waiting on parts occupy a space for weeks
- Inventory, if you flip, occupies spaces permanently
- Employees and customers need spaces
- **You cannot legally store customer cars on the street.** Cities ticket and tow, and
  neighboring tenants will report you.

**Rule of thumb: you need parking for 3x the number of bays you have, minimum.** A
4-bay shop with 6 parking spaces is a shop in permanent crisis. Count spaces before you
count square feet.

### Neighbor and nuisance issues

Your neighbors will determine whether your city visits you. Manage this deliberately.

| Issue | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| **Noise** — impact guns, exhaust, dyno, music | Close the roll-up door when using impact tools. **No engine revving after 6pm, ever.** Check the local noise ordinance for dB limits and hours; industrial zones are more permissive but not unlimited. |
| **Customers loitering** | The single fastest way to become "the problem tenant." Written shop hours, no after-hours hanging out without a reason. |
| **Cars overflowing** | See parking. Never park in a neighbor's spaces, not even once. |
| **Fumes and odor** | Ventilation; no spraying without proper equipment and permits |
| **Fluid stains on shared pavement** | Drip pans, absorbent, clean immediately. Stains are the visible evidence inspectors photograph. |
| **Meets and events** | Talk to neighbors *first*. Hold events on Sundays when industrial neighbors are closed. Hire traffic control if 50+ cars. Get the permit. |

**The move:** in month one, introduce yourself to every adjacent tenant, give them your
cell number, and offer them a discount on service. Neighbors who like you call *you*
when there's a problem. Neighbors who don't know you call the city.

### Choosing between them — the decision rule

**Start mobile (no lease) → industrial unit → add lot capability only when the dealer
license justifies it.**

The industrial unit is the answer for phase 1 because it's the only option that gives
you real capability at a rent you can cover with 2–3 good jobs a week. The small
commercial lot only makes sense once you're licensed and holding inventory, because
you're paying a large premium for outdoor display space you otherwise don't use. The
full dealership lot is a business with a fundamentally different cost structure and
should not be on your roadmap for at least three years.

## 2. Business models — profit and difficulty, honestly assessed

Rated on: **gross margin**, **capital required**, **difficulty to execute well**, and
**how fast it produces cash**.

### The detail

#### ⭐ Detailing — your beachhead
- **Pricing:** maintenance wash $60–120 · full interior/exterior detail $250–450 ·
  paint correction $500–1,500 · engine bay $80–150
- **Cost of goods:** $15–40 in chemicals per car. That's it.
- **Capital:** pressure washer, extractor, polisher, lights, chemicals, canopy,
  containment mat — **$3,000–8,000 fully equipped**
- **Why it's the right start:** no lease required, no license beyond ARD-adjacent
  considerations, immediate cash, and **it builds the customer list that every later
  service sells into.** Every detail customer is a future ceramic, PPF, and service
  customer.
- **The catch:** it's physical, it's time-heavy, and it doesn't scale without hiring.
  You will do 1–2 cars a day yourself. **Treat it as customer acquisition that pays
  you, not as the destination.**
- **Realistic phase 0:** 8–14 cars/week at an average $180 = **$6,000–10,000/month
  gross, $5,000–8,000 net** with no rent.

#### ⭐ Ceramic coating — the best margin-to-effort ratio in this document
- **Pricing:** $800–1,500 (1–2 yr) · $1,500–2,500 (5 yr+) · $2,500–4,000 with full
  correction on a high-end car
- **Material cost:** $80–250. **Margin is 70–85%.**
- **The work is 80% paint correction, 20% coating.** The coating is the easy part. If
  your correction is mediocre, you're sealing in swirls under a 5-year product and
  you'll be dealing with it for 5 years.
- **Training:** $1,500–4,000 for accredited installer certification. Some brands
  (Ceramic Pro, System X, Gtechniq) offer territory or dealer programs at $5,000–15,000
  — evaluate whether the brand's lead generation justifies it. **For most new shops,
  independent certification plus your own marketing beats an expensive territory deal.**
- **Time:** 1–3 days per car. That's the constraint — you can only do 2–4 a week.
- **At 3/week at $1,400 average: $16,800/month gross.** This alone justifies the unit.

#### ⭐ PPF — the highest revenue per bay-hour, and the hardest to learn
- **Pricing:** partial front $700–1,200 · full front $1,200–2,200 · full body
  $5,000–8,500 · track pack $600–1,000
- **Material:** 30–40% of price. Plotter + film + slip solution.
- **Equipment:** plotter $2,500–4,000, pattern software subscription $100–300/mo, heat
  guns, squeegees, a very clean, very well-lit install bay — **dust is the enemy**
- **Difficulty is real.** Full-body PPF is a craft. Expect to ruin $2,000+ of film
  learning. Budget that as tuition, not as failure.
- **Do it after ceramic**, on the same customers, in the same bay.

#### ⭐ Window tint — the perfect attachment sale
- **Pricing:** 2 fronts $80–150 · full car $200–400 · ceramic tint full car $400–800 ·
  windshield strip $80–150
- **Material:** $30–80. Very high margin, fast turnaround (1–2 hrs).
- **⚠️ California tint law is strict and enforced — CVC §26708.** Front side windows
  must allow more than **70% light transmission**. Windshields may only be tinted along
  the top strip (to the AS-1 line / roughly 4 inches). Rear side and rear windows may be
  any darkness **if** the vehicle has dual side mirrors.
- **Do not install illegal tint.** Customers will ask constantly. The correct answer is
  a firm no with an explanation, every time. Illegal installs generate fix-it tickets
  that come back to you, damage your reputation with the customers who matter, and
  create liability. This is a boundary you set on day one.

#### Vinyl wrap
- **Pricing:** full color change $2,500–5,500 · roof/trim $300–800 · chrome delete
  $400–900 · commercial fleet lettering $500–2,000
- **Material:** $700–1,400 per car in premium film (3M 2080, Avery SW900)
- **Equipment:** plotter, clean install bay, heat guns, knifeless tape
- **Skill-gated.** A bad wrap is instantly visible and lifts within weeks. Budget
  $2,000–5,000 for training and materially more for practice panels.
- **The underrated angle:** **commercial fleet wraps and lettering.** Less glamorous,
  far more repeatable, businesses pay net-30 without haggling, and one contractor with
  eight trucks is worth twenty enthusiast one-offs.

#### ⭐ Storage — the most underrated line in this document
- **Pricing:** outdoor covered $100–200/mo · indoor $200–400/mo · premium indoor
  (LA/OC) $350–600/mo
- **Cost:** the space you're already renting. **Margin 80–90%.**
- **Why it matters more than it looks:** 10 cars at $250 = **$2,500/month of recurring
  revenue that covers most or all of your rent before you turn a wrench.** That changes
  the psychology of the entire business — you stop taking bad jobs because you need the
  money.
- **Requirements:** written storage agreement (bailment terms, who insures, lien rights
  for non-payment under California's statutory lien procedures), security, and
  confirmation that storage is a permitted use under your zoning and occupancy.
- **The California angle:** enthusiasts in apartments and HOA neighborhoods have nowhere
  to keep a project car or a seasonal car. **This demand is deep and totally
  underserved.** It's also the natural bridge into the hobby-shop community — your
  storage clients become your bay renters and your event attendees.

#### Maintenance and general repair
- **Labor rate:** $95–165/hr in California, $130–200 for specialty/European
- **Parts margin:** 25–40% on top
- **Reliable, unglamorous base load.** Brakes, fluids, timing belts, suspension.
- Requires **BAR ARD registration**, real diagnostic equipment, and a scanner that
  handles what you service.
- **The strategic point:** maintenance keeps the lights on between the big-ticket
  cosmetic jobs, and it's what makes a customer *yours* rather than a one-time
  transaction.

#### Performance installs — high reward, genuinely high risk
- **Pricing:** intake/exhaust $200–600 labor · coilovers $400–900 · turbo kit
  $2,500–6,000 labor · engine swap $4,000–12,000 labor · tuning $500–1,200
- **Margin 40–55%** — lower than cosmetic work because the hours run over. They always
  run over.
- **⚠️ The three risks that matter:**
  1. **Comebacks.** You installed the kit; the engine fails at 3,000 miles. Whose fault?
     Get a **written agreement before you start** stating what you warrant (your
     workmanship, for a defined period) and what you don't (the customer's parts,
     engine longevity, consequential damage). Have an attorney draft it once.
  2. **Emissions.** Removing or defeating emissions equipment on a street car is illegal
     in California and federally, and CARB actively enforces against shops. **Do not
     install non-CARB-EO parts on street cars, and do not tune out emissions
     equipment.** This has ended shops. Race-only work requires genuine documentation
     that the vehicle is off-road.
  3. **Scope creep and unpaid hours.** Estimate honestly, and remember BAR requires
     written authorization to exceed an estimate.
- Do this only if it's genuinely your specialty and you have the completed-operations
  coverage to back it.

#### Welding and fabrication — the real moat
- **Pricing:** custom exhaust $400–1,200 · roll cage $2,500–6,000 · one-off brackets and
  repairs $100–200/hr · chassis/subframe repair $800–3,000
- **Margin 60–75%** — material cost is low, the value is entirely skill.
- **Equipment:** TIG (entry $900–1,500, professional $4,000+), MIG ($800–2,000), chop
  saw, bender ($1,500–6,000), welding table, plasma cutter ($1,000–2,500)
- **This is the hardest to fake and the hardest to compete with.** If you can TIG
  aluminum and stainless well, you have a business that customers will drive three hours
  for and that no chain can replicate.
- Remember: **fabrication labor is sales-taxable in California** (see doc 01).

#### ⭐ Consignment — phase 3, but note it now
- **Fee:** 8–12% of sale price, or flat $1,500–3,000 per vehicle
- **Capital: essentially zero.** You never own the car.
- **Requires a dealer license.** Consignor funds are trust funds — never operating cash.
- **Why it's excellent:** enthusiast cars are hard to sell privately, sellers hate
  dealing with tire-kickers, and a shop with a reputation and a following is exactly who
  they want handling it. If you've built an audience by then, this converts directly.

#### Car flipping — the honest numbers
Realistic single flip, unlicensed, in California:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase price | $7,000 |
| **Use tax on registration** (~9%) | **–$630** |
| Parts and repairs | –$1,200 |
| Smog + certificate | –$120 |
| Detail supplies | –$60 |
| Registration/transfer fees | –$250 |
| Your labor (20 hrs, unpaid) | –$0 out of pocket |
| **Total in** | **$9,260** |
| Sale price | $11,500 |
| **Gross profit** | **$2,240** |
| **Time:** 3–6 weeks, capital locked | |

Licensed, the same car saves the $630 use tax and gains auction access — where the
purchase price on a comparable car is often **$1,000–2,500 lower** than retail
marketplace. **The same flip licensed produces $3,500–4,500.** That's the whole
argument.

**What actually goes wrong:**
- The car doesn't sell in 3 weeks. Now it's 10 weeks and your capital is dead.
- Hidden damage: transmission, head gasket, frame. Your $2,240 becomes –$1,500.
- Smog failure requiring a CARB-compliant catalytic converter: **–$800 to –$2,000.**
- You get emotionally attached and keep it. (This happens constantly.)

**Rules if you flip:** always pre-purchase inspect. Always pre-smog before buying.
Never buy a car you can't sell in 30 days. Never more than 3 cars at once starting out.
Never buy a salvage title unless that's a deliberate specialty with a dismantler/rebuild
process behind it.

#### DIY bay rental — the thing you want most, do last
- **Pricing:** $40–75/hr per bay · day rate $200–350 · membership $150–400/mo
- **Margin 60–75%** on paper.
- **Every hard problem lives here:** the insurance (see doc 01 — this is a specialty
  policy, not an endorsement), the supervision requirement, tool loss, damage to your
  lifts, weekend-only demand, and members who need help that turns into you working for
  free.
- **Realistic:** 4 bays at 35% weekend-weighted utilization ≈ **$4,000–8,000/month.**
  Compare that to running ceramic and PPF in the same square footage at $16,000+.
- **When it makes sense:** phase 2, in *excess* capacity, with a vetted membership
  drawn from customers you already know, priced as a membership rather than hourly so
  revenue is recurring and predictable.
- **Structure it as a membership club, not an hourly rental.** $199/month for 8 hours of
  bay time, member pricing on service, and event access. Recurring revenue, vetted
  members, and the community forms around it.

#### Tool rental, parts, events
- **Tool rental:** specialty tools only (spring compressors, ball joint press, bearing
  puller, scan tool). $25–75/day. **Requires a deposit and a written checkout log** — you
  will lose tools otherwise. Fine as an add-on to memberships, not a business.
- **Parts:** 15–35% margin, ties up cash, and you cannot compete with RockAuto or
  Amazon on price. **Only stock consumables and the parts you install** (oil, filters,
  brake pads, fluids, hardware), plus a small curated selection for your niche. Special-
  order everything else at a markup for the convenience.
- **Events and meets:** treat as **marketing spend, not revenue.** A monthly Sunday meet
  costs $200–800 (permit, insurance rider, portable restroom, coffee, trash) and is the
  single most effective customer acquisition channel available to you. Charge vendors
  $50–150 per booth to defray. Get the special event permit and the event liability
  rider — a meet where someone does a burnout and hits a spectator is an uninsured
  catastrophe.

## 3. Startup budgets

Five scenarios. Each is internally consistent — equipment, insurance, and runway
together.

**Trim to $25K:** buy a used lift ($2,500–3,500 rather than $5,000), buy used tool boxes,

or insurance.**

**⚠️ The most common fatal error at this level:** spending $23,000 on equipment and
opening with $2,000 in the bank. **You need 3 months of fixed costs in reserve on
opening day, minimum.** If the budget doesn't allow that, get a smaller unit.

**The lesson:** $50,000 buys a serious shop *or* a comfortable runway, not both. Choose
runway.

**At this level the constraint stops being money and becomes management.** You now have
employees, payroll compliance, scheduling, and quality control. Most shops that fail at
$100K fail on operations, not capital.

### What not to buy early — in every budget

| Don't buy | Why | What instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alignment rack** ($18–35K) | Low utilization, huge footprint, long payback | Sublet to a neighbor shop |
| **Paint booth** ($25–60K + AQMD permit) | Permitting alone is months | Subcontract paint |
| **Snap-on tool truck financing** | 2–3x the price, brutal payment terms | Icon/Tekton/Sunex/Harbor Freight pro lines |
| **Dyno** ($40–120K) | Vanity purchase at your scale | Rent time at an existing dyno |
| **A fourth inventory car** | Dead capital | Keep cash |
| **Expensive branding agency** | $8K logo sells nothing | $300 on Fiverr/99designs, spend the rest on content |
| **A large lot before licensing** | Paying for space you can't monetize | Industrial unit |
| **New equipment when used is fine** | Lifts, compressors, tire machines hold up | Auctions, closing shops, Facebook Marketplace |

**The single best equipment strategy in California right now:** shops close constantly.
Watch for closing-shop auctions and estate sales — lifts, compressors, and tire machines
routinely sell at 40–60% of new. Just verify lift condition and get it professionally
installed and ALI-inspected regardless of where it came from.
